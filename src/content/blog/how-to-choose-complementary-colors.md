Complementary colors sit directly opposite one another on the color wheel. Because their wavelengths stimulate opposing photoreceptors in the human retina, pairing them side by side creates immediate visual tension, high contrast, and energetic vibrancy.

## The Classic Complementary Pairs

On the standard 360° color wheel, complementary pairs are separated by 180°:
- **Red & Cyan / Green:** High-intensity contrast used in alerts and seasonal palettes.
- **Blue & Orange:** The classic cinematic "teal and orange" contrast that separates human skin tones from environmental backgrounds.
- **Yellow & Violet / Purple:** Maximum tonal and luminance contrast, providing instant legibility.

## The Common Trap: Vibrating Edges

Placing pure, fully saturated complementary colors directly against one another creates an uncomfortable optical illusion known as chromostereopsis or "vibrating boundaries." The human eye struggles to focus simultaneously on both red and blue light wavelengths.

### How to Fix Vibrating Contrast:
1. **Vary the Lightness:** Make one color significantly darker or lighter than the other (e.g., navy blue paired with soft apricot peach instead of pure cobalt and neon orange).
2. **Desaturate One Shade:** Lower the saturation of the dominant background color to 15%–30%, keeping the accent color vibrant.
3. **Insert a Neutral Buffer:** Separate complementary tones with a crisp white or charcoal divider line.
