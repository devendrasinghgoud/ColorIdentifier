Matching motorcycle paint can be more challenging than matching passenger cars. Motorcycle fuel tanks and racing fairings feature steep compound contours and multi-layer candy finishes that shift in hue depending on lighting and angle.

## Where Motorcycle Manufacturers Hide Paint Codes

Automotive paint codes are uniformly placed on door jambs, but motorcycle manufacturers place their color code labels in varied locations:

- **Yamaha:** Under the rider or passenger seat on a white sticker attached to the subframe (e.g., `0564 / DPBMC` for Deep Purplish Blue Metallic C).
- **Kawasaki:** Under the seat, under the fuel tank, or on the rear fender subframe (e.g., `777` for Lime Green).
- **Ducati:** In the back of the owner's service booklet or stamped near the headstock plate (e.g., `473.101` for Ducati Red / Rosso Corsa).
- **Harley-Davidson:** Often found on the original build sheet, shipping crate label, or matched by year and model name through authorized refinish databases (e.g., `010` for Vivid Black).
- **Honda Motorcycles:** On the frame tube underneath the seat latch or fuel tank bracket (e.g., `R-101C-U` for Candy Glory Red).

## Understanding Motorcycle Multi-Stage Finishes

Many iconic motorcycle colors cannot be duplicated with a simple single-stage enamel:
- **Base Ground Coat:** A silver, white, or gold metallic base that provides reflectivity.
- **Translucent Candy Coat:** A colored transparent glaze that gives depth and saturation.
- **UV Clear Coat:** Protective glossy barrier coat.

Always test sprayouts on a curved test card before applying paint to your actual bike components.
